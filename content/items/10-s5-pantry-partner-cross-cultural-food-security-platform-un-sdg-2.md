## Question
Accessing free food (pantries, community meals) is hard because information is fragmented, outdated or inaccessible, and existing tools put institutional needs ahead of the dignity and trust of the people they serve. How can a location-based tool provide food resources while prioritizing dignity and accessibility?

## Methods
With an international team (Iowa State and Namibia University of Science and Technology): a values workshop mapped to UN SDGs and a team vote; user interviews plus food-insecurity and volunteer surveys; competitive analysis; personas (a new mother in Windhoek; a recently laid-off user with cerebral palsy); a values table with metrics at micro, meso and macro levels; information architecture and a prioritized roadmap; a Figma prototype; and feedback sessions, including a senior product designer.

## Findings
A working Figma prototype with delivery requests for users facing disability or transport barriers, community groups, real-time notifications, and inventory scanning for pantry partners. Feedback praised donation scanning and volunteer tracking and suggested expiry alerts and earlier login. The retrospective named next steps: more iterations, full usability testing, more accessibility features, and checks on data freshness and translation accuracy.
