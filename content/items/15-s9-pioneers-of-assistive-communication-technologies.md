## Question
How have assistive communication technologies evolved, and what makes their design user-centered?

## Methods
Traced PRC-Saltillo's history from a 1966 repurposed-teletype device through portable, head-pointer and touch-screen devices to the LAMP and Unity language systems; evaluated their design against four user-centered design elements (visibility, accessibility, legibility, language), drawing on company history, user testimonials and inclusive-design literature, and on my own experience teaching children to use speech-generating devices.

## Findings
Concluded that PRC-Saltillo sets a standard for user-centered assistive design, and posed open questions for the next generation of AAC: expressing tone and nuance, moving beyond wants and demands, and learning vocabulary as curiosity leads.
