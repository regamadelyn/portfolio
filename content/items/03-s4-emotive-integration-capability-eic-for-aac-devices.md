## Question
AAC devices speak in a flat, monotone voice, so millions of users cannot express emotion, tone or nuance. How can speech-generating devices add emotive prosody, and would that improve users' autonomy, social engagement and behavior?

## Methods
Reviewed literature on AAC outcomes, Functional Communication Training and speech synthesis (unit selection, statistical parametric synthesis, deep neural networks), and selected a hybrid DNN–SPSS framework (Prosody-TTS) for its low data needs. Designed tiered emotion libraries (Fundamental, Intermediate, Advanced; based on Plutchik), a TOME-method use case, a hierarchical task analysis and a UI mockup. Specified data needs (about 56, 112 and 160 hours of transcribed audio per tier), a two-coder agreement protocol for labeling training audio, a listener experiment powered with G*Power (42 ratings per clip; chi-square goodness-of-fit and binomial tests), and a pre/post quasi-experiment with 30+ AAC users rated by two or more clinicians across four domains and analyzed with paired t-tests, plus caregiver and user feedback surveys.

## Findings
A complete research proposal for the Emotive Integration Capability, with its constraints stated (no standard taxonomy for secondary emotions, English-only scope, no individual voice timbre) and an integration path through an open-source AAC platform.
